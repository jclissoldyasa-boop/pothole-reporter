How it works

  1. 1

    Tap the sign

    Phone on the mount, one tap as you pass a pothole. Or your pillion taps. The screen edge flashes green so you know it's logged without looking twice, and red if you missed the button.

  2. 2

    It works out who fixes it

    The app looks up the road, suburb and your direction of travel. Main roads go to the state's road authority, like VicRoads or Transport for NSW. Everything else goes to the right council. It has the contacts for every council in Australia, all 539 of them.

  3. 3

    Send it from your own email

    When you're off the bike, one tap opens a ready-to-send email with the GPS spot, a map link and the time. It goes from you, with your name on it, so councils treat it as a real complaint from a real rider.

Made for riding

Your nav app

Ride with your nav app, side by side

Most Android phones can show two apps at once. Put Google Maps, Waze or your favourite nav app on one half and the pothole sign on the other: directions and one-tap logging on the same screen, and pothole-ahead warnings keep working while the app's on screen.

  1. Open your nav app, then this app.
  2. Open your recent apps (swipe up and hold, or tap the square button).
  3. Tap the icon at the top of one app's card and choose Split screen (Samsung: Open in split screen view). Then pick the other app.
  4. Drag the middle bar to give the map more room. The sign still works at half size.

iPhones don't do split screen, so on an iPhone switch between the apps instead.

    Questions riders ask

    Sending reports

    Who actually gets my report?

    Whoever looks after that road. Freeways, highways and numbered state routes go to the state's road authority, like VicRoads, Transport for NSW or Main Roads WA. Everything else goes to the council for that spot. In Queensland, main-road reports go to the local Transport and Main Roads office for the area. In the ACT, every road goes to the ACT Government.

    Each report shows who it's going to and why. If it's wrong, open Details on the report and pick the other one.

    Why does a button say “website only”?

    Some road authorities don't take reports by email. Transport for NSW, South Australia's Department for Infrastructure and Transport, and the ACT Government only take them by phone or online form, and a few councils are the same. Tap the button and the app shows their phone number and website. If you find an email address that works, you can add it under Council contacts.

    Can I change the email a council's reports go to?

    Yes. Open Council contacts, pick your state, find the council or road authority, and tap Change email. It's saved on your phone only. Use standard puts it back.

    A council wrote back saying it's not their road. Now what?

    Open Sent, find the report and tap Wrong body?. Pick who it should go to, and a copy goes back on your To send list. The email says who you first reported it to and when, so it doesn't look like a double-up. It works the other way too, if a road authority says it's a council road.

    I sent the email but forgot to tap “Yes, mark as sent”.

    No problem. Next time you open the app it asks whether that email went. Until you answer, those potholes are tagged “Emailed, not confirmed” and left out of your next email, so nobody gets the same pothole from you twice.

    Why send from my own email instead of the app sending it?

    A report with a real rider's name on it gets taken more seriously than an automated one, and the council can reply to you directly. Your details only go in emails you choose to send.

    The riders' map

    What does “Still there” do?

    It tells other riders the pothole hasn't been fixed, and keeps it on the map. If another rider logged it, it's also added to your To send list, so the council hears about it from a second rider. Your own potholes aren't added again, and each one is only added once, however often you ride past.

    What's the difference between “Gone” and “Fixed”?

    Gone means properly repaired. “Fixed” means patched, the kind of fix you'll be reporting again after the next rain. Both count towards taking it off the map, and both count in the “fixed” total.

    How does a pothole come off the map?

    When three more riders say it's gone or fixed than say it's still there. If you logged it yourself, tap Mark fixed in Sent and it comes off straight away. Either way it still counts in the totals.

    What does the worst offenders list count?

    Every pothole reported inside each council's area, including ones on main roads. Main-road potholes also show on their own line for each state's road authority.

    Can I keep my potholes off the map?

    Yes. Under Your details, untick Put my potholes on the riders' map. Your reports still work, they just aren't shown to other riders. Deleting a report (swipe it left) also takes it off the map.

    On the bike

    How do the pothole-ahead warnings work?

    When you're moving faster than about 15 km/h, the app checks the road ahead for potholes other riders have logged going your way, and warns you about 8 seconds before you reach one, by voice, beep or silently on screen. Keep the app open with Screen stays on turned on. After you pass, it asks whether the pothole was still there.

    I saw one but couldn't tap in time.

    Use Drop a pin under the riders' map. Search for the road or tap Near me, line the crosshair up with the spot, and add it. It goes in the email as an estimated location, so the council knows to check nearby.

    What if I've got no signal?

    Tap anyway. The spot is saved from GPS, and the road and council are looked up once you're back in range.

    Why doesn't the speed match my speedo?

    It's worked out from GPS, which lags a little and jumps around under trees and in tunnels. It's there so the warnings know you're moving. Ride by your bike's speedo.

    The app

    Does it work on iPhone?

    Yes. Open it in Safari and add it to your home screen. iPhones don't do the Log a pothole shortcut or split screen, but everything else works the same.

    Does it cost anything? Do I need an account?

    It's free, there's no account, and there are no ads.

    Something's not working, or I've got an idea.

    Tap Feedback at the bottom of the app or this page. It opens an email, and every one gets read.

    Your privacy

    What goes on the riders' map

    Only the spot, which way you were going (to the nearest 10°) and the day. You can turn sharing off under Your details in the app.

    What never leaves your phone

    Your name, phone number and email stay on your phone. They only go in the emails you choose to send. No account, no tracking, and no time of day or IP address is stored.

    Looking up the road

    To name the road and who looks after it, the spot is looked up with OpenStreetMap, and in Queensland with the state's QLDTraffic road lookup. Nothing else about you goes with it.
